2. What Streakline is — and what it is not

Streakline is an educational and exploratory fluid dynamics tool. It solves a two-dimensional lattice Boltzmann model on a coarse grid sized to run in real time on a phone.

It is not engineering software, and its output must not be relied upon for engineering, design, certification, safety or commercial decisions.

This is not boilerplate caution. Specific and inherent limitations include:

  • the simulation is two-dimensional; real flows are not
  • the scheme is weakly compressible and isothermal, with no turbulence model; compressibility error grows with the Mach number, which the App displays once it becomes significant
  • the domain is narrow, so blockage effects inflate drag relative to open-air conditions
  • resolution is low by professional standards and boundary layers are under-resolved
  • the top and bottom boundaries are a numerical construct, not a physical wall or open air
  • achievable Reynolds numbers are far below most real applications
  • the solver applies a small artificial perturbation behind the body during start-up to trip the wake into shedding; results reflect that nudge as well as the geometry
  • viscosity is floored for numerical stability, so at high inlet speeds the Reynolds number actually solved is lower than the one requested (the App marks this with a ⚠)

Coefficients reported by the App are indicative of trends and comparisons within the App. They are not validated against experiment, are not a substitute for a qualified CFD study, wind-tunnel testing or professional judgement, and must not be presented as engineering results.

You are solely responsible for any decision you make on the basis of output from the App.
